Model United Nations Instructor Resource Index

This page organizes the PSIR 172 MUN package into one place so it can actually be used in class. It covers preparation, assignments, live simulation support, and post-simulation reflection for the class MUN on International Responsibility-Sharing for Refugees and Displaced Persons.

1. Core Presentation and Orientation

MUN Introduction Deck

Week 9 · Background + format explanation

HTML slide deck covering the UN, UN organs, MUN formats, why the class is using a GA-style humanitarian simulation, and how the position paper fits into the exercise.

Open Slide Deck

Google Form: Delegation Preferences

Pre-assignment workflow

Use the previously drafted Google Form to collect country preferences, partner requests, and confidence/skill information before assigning delegations.

Use the text draft in your planning documents to build the form in Google Forms.

Country Assignment Master Sheet

Instructor-side allocation tool

The master delegation list for the 18 countries, student pairings, country role types, and notes on assignment logic.

Best stored in Excel or Google Sheets for live tracking.
2. Student Preparation Materials

Position Paper Assignment Sheet

Delegation submission guidance

Full assignment brief explaining what a position paper is, what it should include, expected length, research expectations, and assessment criteria.

Delegation Briefing Template

Country research bridge document

One-page structured guide helping students identify national interests, allies, tensions, supported policies, red lines, and delegation strategy.

MUN Position Paper Builder

Interactive HTML tool

Browser-based builder for country position, interests, allies, red lines, supported solutions, opening speech, and generated paper draft.

Open Builder App

Opening Speech Builder

Speech preparation worksheet

Guides students in converting their country research into a 45–75 second formal opening statement using diplomatic language.

Resolution-Drafting Worksheet

From ideas to committee text

Helps students distinguish preambulatory and operative clauses, identify bloc language, and draft workable committee proposals.

Student Rules Handout

Simulation quick-reference sheet

One-page rules summary for students covering recognition by the Chair, speaking as the state, caucusing, realistic proposals, and diplomatic conduct.

3. Simulation Day Support

Week 10 Simulation Plan

Simulation Part I

Covers opening, roll call, opening speeches, moderated discussion, caucusing, and initial draft resolution work.

Week 11 Simulation Plan

Simulation Part II

Covers clause negotiation, draft consolidation, informal amendments, final debate, consensus/voting, and debrief.

Chair Script + Simplified Rules

Instructor live-use guide

Provides ready-made chair language for opening the committee, recognizing speakers, moving to caucus, handling draft clauses, amendments, and decisions.

Printable Placards

Desk labels for delegations

Printable folded country placards for all 18 delegations to improve room structure, visibility, and simulation atmosphere.

Open Placards

Country Release Template

Student-facing assignment notice

Clean announcement template for releasing delegation assignments, reminding students of their role, next tasks, and simulation purpose.

Participation Tracking Tables

Instructor monitoring

Supports informal assessment of speaking, caucusing, drafting, and delegation participation across Weeks 10 and 11.

4. Post-Simulation Reflection and Closure

Post-MUN Reflection Sheet

Individual debrief task

Reflection prompts asking students to analyze country representation, negotiation, compromise, outcomes, and lessons about diplomacy and international cooperation.

Reflection Rubric

Simple 5-point model

Light-touch rubric for evaluating reflection quality, insight into representation, negotiation analysis, learning, and clarity.

5. Suggested Teaching Sequence

Week 9

  • Housekeeping and transition from midterm period
  • UN and MUN background
  • Explain alternative MUN formats
  • Explain why the class uses a GA-style humanitarian committee
  • Release or prepare delegation assignments
  • Introduce position paper and country research

Weeks 10–11

  • Opening speeches and formal discussion
  • Caucusing and bloc formation
  • Resolution drafting and revisions
  • Amendments, consensus test, or vote
  • Debrief and post-simulation reflection
