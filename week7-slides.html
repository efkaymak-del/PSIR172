Debrief: "Hello Türkiye"

ElementAnalysis
Target AudienceInternational media, diplomats, tourism sector — not Turkish citizens
FramingPositive — pride, identity, modernity
PersuasionEthos (government authority) + Pathos (national pride)

Key lesson: The name change itself was the message. The campaign turned a bureaucratic request into a story the media wanted to tell.

Professionalisation of Campaigns

McNair's key argument: political communication has become a professional industry.

  • Pollsters, media consultants, data analysts, PR strategists
  • Communication is planned, tested, and disciplined — not improvised
  • Even "spontaneous" moments are often scripted
  • Every clip we watched today was the product of a professional team
For your group project: You don't need a big budget. But you DO need a clear strategy before you choose a medium.

Paid vs. Earned vs. Owned Media

Paid

You buy placement. TV ads, sponsored posts, billboards. Full control, but everyone knows it's an ad.

Earned

Others tell your story. News coverage, shares, word of mouth. Most credible — but you can't control it.

Owned

Your platforms. Website, social accounts, newsletter. You control message and timing, but limited reach.

The sweet spot: Use owned media to create something newsworthy → earn media coverage → amplify with paid if needed.

Negative Campaigning

Direct Attack

Target the opponent's record with facts. "They voted against X three times."

Contrast

Compare your position vs. theirs. "We support X; they oppose it."

Fear Appeal

Emotional threat without specific evidence. "If they win, everything changes."

Boomerang effect: If the attack seems unfair, voters sympathise with the target and turn against the attacker. This is why contrast ads are safer than personal attacks.

TimeCover this

1:30–2:15Message — What's your key message? What frame?
2:15–3:00Medium — How will you deliver it? What tools?
Use the feedback form on the session app to give each group a quick note.

Workshop: Sharpen Your Strategy (10 min)

In your groups, answer these three questions on the session app:

1. Audience

Who exactly? Demographics, values, media habits. NOT "everyone."

2. Message

One sentence. If they remember nothing else, what do they remember?

3. Medium

Primary delivery method. Why this one? Paid, earned, or owned?

Section A: Multiple Choice

  • 8 questions, 4 options each, 1 mark per question
  • Covers Weeks 1–7: research methods, data, policy writing, framing, persuasion, media
  • Mix of definition questions and applied scenarios
Tip: Read ALL four options before answering. Eliminate obviously wrong answers first. Don't spend more than 2 minutes on any one question.

Section B: Short Analysis

  • Read a short passage (~100 words)
  • Question 1 (2 marks): Identify the framing type. Cite specific words.
  • Question 2 (2 marks): Identify persuasion modes (ethos, pathos, logos). Give examples from the text.
Tip: Always quote specific words or phrases from the passage. "The passage uses negative framing, as seen in the phrase '...' " gets full marks. Vague answers don't.

Section C: Applied Scenario

You receive a scenario. Write a campaign communication plan (300–400 words).

PartWhat to writeMarks
(a) Target AudienceSpecific group, demographics, why them2
(b) Framing StrategyPositive, negative, or both — explain + example message2
(c) Key Message3–4 sentences using ≥2 persuasion modes2
(d) Ethical AwarenessOne risk + how to avoid it2
Common mistake: vague audience. "Young people" = weak. "University students aged 18–25 in Northern Cyprus who use Instagram daily" = strong.

Practice: Section C (15 min)

Scenario: A local animal shelter is struggling. Fewer people are adopting pets, and the shelter is running out of space and funding. The shelter director has asked you to design a short communication campaign to encourage more adoptions.

Write your answer now — parts (a), (b), (c), and (d). You have 15 minutes.

Use the session app or write on paper. This is practice — not graded.

Model Answer: Key Points

PartStrong Answer Includes
(a) AudienceSpecific: "Young adults 20–30, students, near the shelter, active on social media." Explains why this group.
(b) FramingBoth: negative opening (urgency, shelter capacity), positive close (rewarding action). Gives example phrases.
(c) MessageUses logos (47 animals, vaccinated), ethos (vet's quote), pathos (emotional language). Labels each mode.
(d) EthicsNames a specific risk (guilt-tripping, distressing images). Says how to avoid it (hopeful imagery, factual urgency).
Scoring logic: Each part is 0–2 marks. 0 = missing or irrelevant. 1 = partially correct or vague. 2 = specific, accurate, and well-explained.
